# Phase 6 — creator-local appointments and adversarial account/payment recovery

**Status: open; not release-ready.** Reviewed September 27, 2026, starting from `3712e20b331ce7d255280595869f0ba01b1c7112` on `codex/phase-1-foundation`. The user selected **each creator's local time zone**. This report distinguishes application corrections, verification of the new implementation, and remaining hosted/operational gates. No production migrations, payments, refunds, emails, account deletion or deployment were performed.

## Findings and corrections

| ID | Priority | Reproduced behavior | Correction and regression evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| P6-01 | P1 | UTC dates and the visitor's calendar could accept elapsed appointments, reject future local ones, permit late deposits, and misclassify reviews/reminders. A pending appointment could still be confirmed after its start. | Creator explicitly saves an IANA zone with all seven working days in one transaction. Each new appointment snapshots its zone/start/end instants. SQL validates actual start, horizon, local working hours, duration, overlap and status changes. Deposit, review, reminder and history behavior uses saved instants. Unknown historical zones fail closed. `booking-timezones`, `booking-zone-independent`, `booking-ui`, browser booking tests. |
| P6-02 | P1 | Shared client health/booking notes remained available to a creator after the appointment actually ended in an eastern time zone. | The RLS helper now requires an actual future end and an active participant booking; own access, sharing disabled, stranger, cancellation and actual-ended controls are tested under real roles. |
| P6-03 | P2 | The booking screen required following a private creator, but a direct authenticated insert bypassed that requirement. | The database enforces the same creator/follower requirement while holding the creator lock. No row is created by a forbidden request; a follower's valid booking still succeeds. |
| P6-04 | P1 | A refund provider lookup could fail before the deletion barrier was recorded. An older worker could clear uncertainty introduced by a newer event. | Mark the accepted local payment before external reads; fence generic and late-deposit workers with event/claim revisions. Durable review blocks deletion until a valid current reconciliation succeeds. `payment-recovery`. |
| P6-05 | P1 | Valid refund histories exceeding one page could never reconcile. A freshly retrieved failed refund and a stale successful list could be accepted as settled. | Read and validate complete paginated history, reject missing/duplicate/nonadvancing entries and inconsistent snapshots, and retry with the review barrier retained. Bound processing at 2,000 refunds; larger histories require operator review. |
| P6-06 | P1 | Clearing mutable PaymentIntent metadata caused known credit and late-deposit refund events to be acknowledged without applying their financial outcome. | Bind reconciliation to already accepted ledger ownership. Absent metadata cannot erase that identity; conflicting present identity still fails. Real SQL verifies balances and refund obligations. `refund-independent`. |
| P6-07 | P1 | Same-second subscription events and repeated event/checkout deliveries could retain or restore stale paid access even after the current provider state became unpaid. | Acquire a fenced subscription lease before reading the provider, then apply that current state. Event receipts do not bypass a new authoritative read; exact previously bound checkout identity can reconcile after its attempt is consumed. Renewal credit grants retain their separate once-only ledger. `subscription-contracts`, `subscription-independent`. |
| P6-08 | P1 | Removing subscription metadata prevented a verified owner's cancellation and left terminal checkout attempts blocking recovery/deletion. | Prefer durable subscription/customer/owner bindings; release only the exact terminal attempt after current provider verification. Mismatched customers and unrelated attempts remain forbidden. |
| P6-09 | P1 | Retrying an ambiguous subscription create after a configured Price change reused its idempotency key with different parameters. Fulfillment and renewal of recognized older Prices then failed. | Freeze the original Price with the attempt and persist exact server-recognized Price-to-plan mappings. Conflicting remaps and never-recognized Prices fail closed. Historical checkout, renewal and return-page outcomes are tested. |
| P6-10 | P1 | An unpaid/paused/incomplete subscription with an unrecognized Price could leave its previous paid tier in place while returning an error. | Non-entitled statuses revoke access without inventing a plan. Active/trialing/past-due access still requires a recognized exact Price. |
| P6-11 | P1/P2 | Financial and deletion forms could use another account's freshly read token, redirect a new account after an old response, or sign out a new session during deletion cleanup. | Shared actions bind identity before and after awaited work, reject duplicate submissions and invalidate A→B→A work. Deletion refreshes the latest stored session under the installed SDK lock. Tests include the real SDK's session-storage/lock behavior. `auth-actions`, `deletion-session-sdk`. |
| P6-12 | P2 | OAuth could lose referral information and fail without a visible retryable error. | Preserve the referral and current origin through OAuth; surface provider errors and allow retry. `oauth-onboarding`. |
| P6-13 | P2 | Appointment screens retained private state or late redirects across account changes; zero-row writes could claim that an action succeeded. Review rewards could read a later account's token. | Account-owned screen remounts and canceled initialization reads prevent stale state/redirects. Actions require returned rows and use the submitting account's captured token. `booking-auth-independent`. |
| P6-14 | P2 | Failed availability reads could present editable default hours and overwrite the real schedule; direct table writes bypassed atomic validation. | Fail visibly with retry before enabling the form; require the authenticated service endpoint and atomic SQL function. Browser roles retain reads but cannot write availability directly. `availability-api-independent`, `booking-auth-independent`, actual-role SQL tests. |

During implementation, independent tests also caught and corrected an ambiguous SQL output-column reference, omitted midnight-ending slots, midnight displayed as noon, a planner Today/week mismatch, and stale slot responses. ## Real services and test doubles

SQL verification executes the migrations, locks, transactions, triggers, constraints and RLS against native PostgreSQL. `anon` and `authenticated` do not have BYPASSRLS. The local catalog-derived fixture is not a complete managed Supabase clone. PGlite is useful for fast checks but serializes operations; final concurrency evidence uses native PostgreSQL connections and explicit lock races.

Route/component tests use injected Stripe, Auth transport and PostgREST doubles. Actual installed Supabase Auth lock/storage and Stripe signature checks complement them. Browser tests render the production Next build in Chromium and mobile WebKit with external services intercepted. They verify SDK request payloads and visible outcomes, not a deployed card payment or hosted database write. No passing mock is described as a successful live refund or email.

Stripe events are not a reliable ordered stream; leases serialize fresh reads and fence stale workers. Historical Prices are accepted only if the server has explicitly recognized them. PostgreSQL can otherwise resolve repeated/nonexistent local times implicitly; this implementation rejects ambiguous/invalid appointment starts and DST-crossing duration mismatches instead. [Stripe event ordering](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks#event-ordering), [PostgreSQL date/time functions](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/functions-datetime.html).

## Remaining release gates

1. **No confirmed isolated hosted environment.** The last verified Supabase target was production `faunikvhoommbebsmevg`, without a branch; Vercel Preview shared Production variables. The user did not know of staging. Stripe account discovery was retried in Phase 6 and still requires reauthentication. Dashboard access was previously blocked by an administrator-policy verification failure; it was not bypassed. No paid resource was provisioned.
2. **Managed end-to-end verification and restore remain open.** Use an isolated Supabase project, Stripe sandbox, staging-only app variables/redirects and synthetic users to verify checkout, 3DS/delayed settlement, renewals, refunds, Auth, Storage, deletion and backup restore. A production-backed Preview does not satisfy this gate.
3. **Timezone migration requires operator reconciliation.** Migration 014 does not guess creator zones or reinterpret old appointments. Creators must explicitly save their zone; historical active appointments with unknown instants block new booking/payment and do not expose shared health notes, send reminders or authorize reviews. Reconcile them against verified creator-local dates before rollout. Existing appointments with saved zones keep their original instants after settings change. No automatic historical backfill was applied.
4. **Rehearse coordinated migrations 001–015 before deploying dependent code.** New RPCs and columns are required. Migration 014 revokes direct availability writes; migration 015 revokes unfenced subscription entry points. Old clients/workers may receive failures during an uncoordinated rollout. The application must not be deployed independently of the verified migration plan.
5. **Historical finance and review states require operations.** Reconcile pre-migration ambiguous checkout attempts whose original Price is unknown, never-recognized historical Prices, purchases, held credits, subscription periods, boosts and refunds. Provider histories above 2,000 refunds and paginated/ambiguous invoices remain explicit review cases. Supply monitoring, replay/reconciliation procedures and an operator workflow; no fabricated financial adjustment policy was added.
6. **Worker cadence and exact-code CI remain open.** Configure secrets, supported reminder/refund recovery cadence and alerts; the existing daily cron is not proof of timely retry. Run the complete new-code Ubuntu workflow after publication. Local native results do not establish OS parity or deployed service behavior.

Phase 6 remains open while these verification and rollout gaps remain.
